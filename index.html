Job Market Paper
The Informal Safety Net: Extended Families, Intra-Household Bargaining, and Female Labor Force Participation in China
PDF Online Appendix
Extended families are a central source of childcare in China, yet living with the husband's parents may also shift bargaining power within the couple. I study how family structure shapes the child penalty in women's labor force participation. Using event-study estimates of the child penalty, I address the endogeneity of co-residence with a control function that exploits variation in One-Child Policy enforcement intensity interacted with sibling count. I then estimate a structural search-and-matching model of the marriage market in which couples jointly bargain over residential arrangement, the wife's participation and the allocation of consumption and time. The wife's estimated Nash bargaining weight is 0.52 in nuclear households and 0.46 in extended households: co-residence provides childcare that supports participation, but at the cost of the wife's bargaining position.

Working Papers

Nonparametric Identification of the Household Pareto Weight from Labor Supply
PDF
I show how the household Pareto weight can be nonparametrically identified from spouses' labor supply, wages and total household consumption. I apply the identification result to data from the Japanese Panel Survey of Consumers (JPSC).
